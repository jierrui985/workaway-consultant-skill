# Host analysis

## Extract the evidence

Track each item as **Field | Content | Evidence/source | Status**:

- Country, city, region, and precision of the public location; never infer a private address.
- Family/homestay, hostel, farm, community, education, or environmental type; a mixed project can have a main and secondary type. Record only visible household/host count, response rate, rating, recent activity, and capacity.
- Project purpose, daily tasks, and whether work relates to a business or professional duties.
- Hours per day, workdays per week, total hours, shifts, days off, commute, minimum stay, and available dates as separate fields. If absent, write **Not stated**. Days spent living with a family are not hours worked per day.
- Required language, experience, credentials, physical demands, independence, driving, and tool use.
- Private or shared room, bathroom, internet, number of meals, meals on days off, pets, smoking, and dietary restrictions.
- Free accommodation, food actually provided, any wages or food allowance, the volunteer's own meals and necessary costs, deposits, transfers, equipment, and training. A food allowance is not necessarily three provided meals.
- Dates and number of reviews actually visible, including repeated positives and negatives. Old reviews do not establish current conditions.

Flag cropped screenshots, stale pages, and conflicting claims. Do not replace a specific host promise with a general platform rule. When the listing and a review disagree, show both with dates and sources and mark the point **To confirm**.

## Interpret the host's needs

Separate **Explicit requirement** from **Inference**. For example, a listing about daily guest reception may suggest that clear communication and reliable attendance matter; it does not prove a volunteer must run a night shift alone. Link any personality trait to a specific working arrangement. Do not infer ability from nationality, age, or gender.

Translate each task into what a useful contribution looks like and what real applicant evidence supports it. Separate hard requirements such as credentials, minimum stay, and dates from preferences that can be learned.

## Verify decision-changing conditions

Prioritize actual hours and rest, food and room coverage, mandatory charges, unsupervised duties, the final transfer, and whether a place is still available. For professional or higher-risk tasks, clarify training, qualifications, and responsibility; do not assume the applicant can perform them.

Name concerns with their sources. Few reviews, a remote location, or a short description alone do not prove fraud or danger; strong ratings must not hide specific contrary evidence.
