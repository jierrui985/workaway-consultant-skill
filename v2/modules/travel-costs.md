# Travel route and costs

## Establish the basis

Determine the actual departure city, destination area, dates or range, number of travelers, luggage, and one-way/return basis. Without dates, outline the route but do not give a trip-date quote. Without a departure point, do not give a complete arrival total; still assess local legs where possible.

Compare one recommended practical route with meaningful alternatives, such as flights, trains, or buses, rather than listing every possible mode. Consider fare, total elapsed time, connections, luggage, transfers, and fatigue.

## Break down the door-to-door trip

Include departure home/city to terminal, long-distance travel, arrival terminal to city where applicable, city to project, and the final bus, pickup, or taxi. If the project is near the arrival terminal, use the direct route instead of inventing a detour through downtown.

Use local time zones. Show overnight arrivals, transfer buffers, and last services. The sum of ride times is not necessarily the complete door-to-door duration. If a connection cannot be verified, mark it **To confirm**.

## Price and conversion

- Label each price: trip-date quote, dated reference fare, evidence-based estimate, or not yet checked. An advertisement for a different month is not a purchasable quote for the user's dates. Give the access date and the date the price actually covers.
- Check taxes, luggage, booking fees, terminal transfers, night surcharges, and overnight accommodation. List unknowns separately; they are not zero.
- State the exchange-rate direction, source, and access date: `1 unit of local currency = r units of preferred currency`. Multiply the original amount by `r`; the user may request CNY. Conversion is indicative and payment rates may differ.
- Convert both bounds of a range separately. Retain precision internally but round the displayed result to match the evidence; do not show cents for a rough estimate.
- Total per-person costs before multiplying by group size. Divide a per-car taxi fare appropriately rather than charging every passenger the full amount.
- Do not double a return fare already covering both directions. Convert different currencies separately before summing. Treat a pickup as free only when explicitly confirmed.

## Totals and limits

Show the recommended route's known partial subtotal, any evidence-based estimate, unpriced legs, and exclusions. If any necessary leg is unpriced, call the result a **partial subtotal**, never the complete travel budget.

Separate local living costs, explicit project charges, necessary connection accommodation, and any requested additional items. A discretionary contingency is a budget assumption, not a sourced cost. Do not count it or a transit leg twice. Include insurance, membership, or entry fees only when relevant and grounded in reliable evidence; otherwise mark **To confirm**.

If exchange rates cannot be checked, retain original amounts and the conversion formula, without a fabricated converted total. If there is no price evidence, use **To check**, not a plausible-looking range.
